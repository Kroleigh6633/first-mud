# World: Aeldran

## Overview

Aeldran is a Celtic/Welsh-flavored northern continent — a land of moors, highland kingdoms, deep temperate forests, cold rocky coasts, and drowned ruins offshore. The south warms into older, more cosmopolitan territories. Beneath it all runs a buried skeleton of a civilization that mastered magic and fell.

The people of Aeldran live in the long shadow of what came before.

| **The Gravenmarsh** | Boggy midlands. Artifact hunters, scholars, wanderers. | Gravenhold — headquarters of the Gravenguard |

### The Road Network

Seven major roads connect the regions — the King's Roads, built during an earlier unified age. Travel between regions requires either:
- Walking the roads (time, danger, encounter rolls)
- Earning reputation with a faction that controls waypoints (reduced danger)
- Unlocking portal anchors (endgame fast travel — earned, never bought)

## History

### The Age of the Ardweld

Thousands of years before the current age, a magocracy called the **Ardweld** unified the continent. They mastered all five magical elements simultaneously — the only civilization to achieve this. They built:

- Continental ward-networks (communication, weather control)
- Ward-engines (magical automata — golems, constructs, sentinels)
- Dravenite refinement techniques (lost in the Sundering)
- Pocket dimensions and planar anchors (the portals the player will eventually unlock)
- The Ardweld Remnant — deliberate refuge dimensions, created before the end

They did not fall gradually. They ended in a single event.

### The Sundering

The exact cause is unknown. Every faction has a theory:
- **House Caervorn** says the Ardweld overreached magic — proof that magic destroys
- **The Thornwood Covens** say the Ardweld used Unmaking without discipline — a cautionary tale, not a condemnation of magic itself
- **The Gravenguard** believes the Sundering was weaponized — done *to* the Ardweld by something from outside
- **The Ashen Court** claims the Ardweld succeeded — that the Sundering was transformation, not destruction, and the Ashen Court are the inheritors

No theory is entirely wrong. The truth is locked in the Ardweld Remnant.

### The Long Dark (0 — 400 AS)

After the Sundering. No magical infrastructure. Dravenite veins cracked and dangerous. Ward-engines running without purpose, sometimes hostile. Settlements rebuilt around ruins. The Wildfolk proliferated as Weave-saturated areas became their territory.

### The Fractured Age (400 — 900 AS)

Kingdoms formed and collapsed. The Thornwood Covens preserved magical knowledge. The Golvari retreated underground permanently. The Fairgean closed their shores. The Compact Cities emerged as neutral ground.

### The Current Age (900 AS — Present: 1,247 AS)

A fragile stability. Seven major powers coexist through tension rather than peace. The Ashen Court has been quiet for thirty years. Nobody believes they will stay that way.

The player arrives at this moment.

## The Current Year: 1,247 AS

### Political State

- **House Caervorn** is arming. A new lord — Lord-Marshal Aldric Caervorn — is young, ambitious, and believes consolidating Aeldran under Caervorn rule is divine mandate.
- **The Thornwood Covens** know about Aldric's plans. They are preparing, quietly.
- **The Compact** wants no war. War closes roads. Closed roads kill trade. They are funding everyone's intelligence networks simultaneously.
- **The Gravenguard** recently lost an expedition in the Ardweld Remnant. Three Honored members, gone. Nobody is saying what they found.
- **The Fairgean** have been seen inland. This has not happened in two centuries.
- **The Golvari** sent an ambassador to Gravenhold six months ago. Nobody knows what was said.
- **The Ashen Court** has been silent. Lord-Assessor Maren (a Soul-Touched nightmare) was last seen nine months ago.

Into this — a Rider arrives with an uncanny horse and a brooch of unknown origin.

---

## The Player — A Rider

Players begin as **Riders** — king's messengers. The role is not glamorous. The roads are dangerous. The pay is inconsistent.

But Riders have three things others do not:
1. A **horse** that is smarter than it should be and cannot be lost permanently
2. A **brooch** of Ardweld origin that responds to the Weave — the player's magical focus and anchor
3. **Access** — Riders cross faction lines by necessity. Everyone talks to a Rider. This is the reputation seed.

The player's wyrd is unknown at start. Their magical affinity has not yet manifested. Their first quest is a delivery gone wrong.

Everything follows from that.
