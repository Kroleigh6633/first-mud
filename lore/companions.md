# Companion System

## Overview

Companions are found, earned, summoned, or captured. They are never purchased from a shop. Each companion has depth that only reveals itself through sustained use — a companion you bench learns nothing, and a companion you bench for long enough will drift.

The system draws from three sources:
- **Summoners War**: Monster capture, evolution, elemental skill trees, combat fielding
- **Baldur's Gate**: Personal quest lines, personality, moral reactions, relationship depth
- **Asheron's Call**: Use-based skill advancement, no guaranteed progression path

---

## Core Principle: Use It or Lose It

Every companion skill has a **usage counter** and a **drift rate**. Skills that are actively used advance toward the next layer. Skills that are neglected drift back — not catastrophically, but noticeably.

This is not punishing. A companion you haven't used in 40 hours is still your companion and still useful at their current level. Their advanced skills simply need re-engagement before they push further. The message is: the relationship requires maintenance.

---

## Ability Layers

Each companion has **6 ability layers**. Layer 1 is visible immediately. The rest are locked.

| Layer | Unlock Condition | Nature |
|-------|-----------------|--------|
| 1 | Acquired | Basic abilities — the companion as you first meet them |
| 2 | 20+ hours active use | Deeper skill expression. The companion's real personality begins to show. |
| 3 | Specific usage threshold + 1 personal quest | A revelation about their past or nature. A new ability category unlocked. |
| 4 | Relationship depth (moral choices matter) | They start acting on your behalf without being asked. Abilities extend beyond combat. |
| 5 | Companion's personal wyrd quest completed | They reveal who they actually are. Major ability unlock. The story pays off. |
| 6 | Joint wyrd entanglement resolved | Their fate and yours are recognized as linked. Final ability form. Not always combat-focused. |

Layer 5 and 6 are narrative events as much as mechanical unlocks. A companion at Layer 6 is a character, not a tool.

## Companion Types

---

### Wildfolk
*Elemental Spirits — Found, Not Summoned*

**Source**: Manifest spontaneously when a mage's Weave resonates in a location. The player cannot force this — they create conditions, the Wildfolk chooses.

**Nature**: Not human-intelligent but deeply aware. Each aligns to one element. They communicate through sensation, not language (early layers). By Layer 4 they communicate clearly.

**Personality**: Consistent with their element — Fire Wildfolk are impulsive and passionate, Water are patient and memory-oriented, Earth are steady and cautious, Air are curious and distractible, Aether are uncanny and know things they shouldn't.

**Layer progression**:
- L1: Combat presence (elemental minor effect in their element)
- L2: Weave sharing (reduces Weave cost for their element)
- L3: Elemental sense (warn of threats in their element — Fire Wildfolk smells smoke from far away)
- L4: Communication unlocked (first real conversation — often a revelation)
- L5: Wyrd-sight (they can see the player's wyrd clearly and will tell them what they see)
- L6: Elemental merger (temporary — player can express the Wildfolk's element as if it were their primary affinity)

**Relationship rule**: Wildfolk leave if the player repeatedly uses Aether/Unmaking in their presence without cause. Recovery quest available in the Wyrd-Paths.

**Notes**: A mage can have multiple Wildfolk but they interact with each other — sometimes well, sometimes not. An Air Wildfolk and an Earth Wildfolk will be in persistent low-grade tension.

---

### Hired Heroes
*Human/Golvari/Fairgean NPCs — Earned Through Reputation*

**Source**: Specific reputation threshold + a quest to find and recruit them. Not available from notice boards. The player hears about them through other quests, faction interactions, or the world.

**Nature**: Full characters with history, agenda, and opinions. They agreed to travel with the player for their own reasons, which may not be immediately clear.

**Personality**: Varies by individual. Some are mercenary (straightforward). Some are running from something. Some are pursuing something. Layer 5 reveals which category they're actually in.

**Layer progression**:
- L1: Their stated skills — what they told you they could do
- L2: A secondary skill they didn't mention (players discover this through use)
- L3: Their personal quest becomes available
- L4: Their real motivation is hinted at (ambiguous, interpretable)
- L5: Personal quest concludes — their actual history revealed
- L6: They make a choice based on who the player is, not what the player needs — this choice is permanent and varies by relationship history

**Relationship rule**: Companions react to faction choices. A Thornwood mage companion will not accompany the player into Caervorn territory without a conversation first. A Golvari companion will not work with a player who has done significant anti-Golvari quests. These are not silent — the companion will say something before the limit is reached.

**Breaking point**: Ignore a companion's clear warning three times on the same issue and they leave. Recovery requires a specific personal quest — harder than keeping them.

---

### Captured Monsters
*The Summoners War Layer*

**Source**: Combat capture mechanic — certain monsters can be captured rather than killed. Requires a capture item (crafted, not purchased). Success rate depends on: monster health, player skill, monster type.

**Nature**: Not sapient in the Hired Hero sense. More like deeply bonded animals with magical ability. Their personality is breed-consistent with individual variation.

**Combat role**: This is the Summoners War layer — fielding a party of monsters in turn-based combat. Element vs element, skill activation, evolution tiers.

**Evolution system**:
- Monsters have 3 evolution tiers
- Evolving requires: specific materials, the monster at level cap for their current tier, and a crafting step
- Each evolution changes visual representation (ASCII art style update) and unlocks new abilities
- Some evolutions have branching paths — two different tier-2 forms depending on how the monster was leveled

**Layer progression** (adapted for monsters):
- L1: Basic combat abilities
- L2: Second ability slot unlocked
- L3: Elemental synergy ability (interacts with other party members' elements)
- L4: Evolution tier 2 available
- L5: Third ability slot, signature move
- L6: Evolution tier 3 — final form, passive always-on ability

**Notes**: Some monsters can only be found in specific worlds. A monster native to the Golvari Deeps cannot be captured in Aeldran. Cross-world monster teams become possible mid-game.

---

### Bound Shades
*Dangerous — Aether/Unmaking Research Unlock*

**Source**: A specific research branch and a quest involving Aether/Unmaking Tier 4. Not available earlier.

**Nature**: The remnant of a will — not a ghost exactly, not an echo. Something that was a person and chose to remain. They are aware of what they gave up and have opinions about it.

**Danger**: A Bound Shade can turn if: the player's Weave drops critically low (they feel the power imbalance), the player acts against their pre-binding agreements, or their own wyrd-tangle reaches a threshold.

**Power**: Exceptional. Late-game. A Bound Shade at Layer 5 is among the most powerful entities the player can field. The risk-reward calculus is real.

**Layer progression**:
- L1: Their initial ability — partial, constrained
- L2: Their history (they remember being alive and will share pieces)
- L3: Second ability unlocked + a warning about their limits
- L4: They tell the player their turning conditions explicitly (fair play — they value honesty)
- L5: Their final ability — tied to who they were in life
- L6: A choice: they can be released (they move on, gratefully, and the ability is lost) or remain (permanently bound, fully powered, and they are genuinely content with this)

---

### Ardweld Constructs
*No Personality Layer — Pure Utility*

**Source**: Ardweld Remnant world quests. Not available in Aeldran.

**Nature**: Ward-engines — automated magical constructs built by the Ardweld. No personality. No agenda. No wyrd. They do not betray, drift, leave, or develop feelings. They also do not grow.

**Advantage**: Utterly reliable within their parameters. Never surprises. Never complications.

**Disadvantage**: What you see at acquisition is what you have. Minor improvements through Ardweld research unlocks. No Layer depth — the six layers are replaced by a simple upgrade tree.

**Notes**: Some players will love these. They are the "I just want the system to work" option. The game respects this choice without penalizing it.

## Multi-Companion Dynamics

When multiple companions travel together, they interact:

| Pairing | Result |
|---------|--------|
| Two Wildfolk of opposing elements | Low-grade tension — periodic minor negative effects, manageable |
| Two Wildfolk of the same element | Resonance — amplified elemental effects for both |
| Hired Hero + their faction enemy | Will not work together until a specific quest resolves the conflict |
| Captured Monster + its natural predator species (captured) | Requires significant relationship depth with both to keep stable |
| Bound Shade + any Wildfolk | Wildfolk are deeply uncomfortable. Aether Wildfolk are fascinated. |
| Two Hired Heroes with overlapping history | Discovered at Layer 3 of one of them — creates a joint quest |

---

## Companion Limit

Active party: 3 companions at once in combat.
Roster: Unlimited (companions who aren't active wait — they don't drift as fast but still drift slightly).
Base assignment: Companions can be assigned to base automation tasks when not traveling — they contribute to production based on their skills.
